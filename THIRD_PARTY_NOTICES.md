# Geographic data

Automatic city time zones use [city-timezones](https://github.com/kevinroberts/city-timezones) v1.3.4 (npm package declares MIT) and [tz-lookup](https://github.com/darkskyapp/tz-lookup) v6.1.25 (CC0). City points are approximate city centers, not venue coordinates. The city list is finite and remains available if worldwide search is unavailable. IANA/Intl rules apply daylight saving to dates, rather than storing fixed UTC offsets.

Google Maps and Places data is loaded only through Google's APIs when configured. Place IDs may be retained; provider names, addresses, coordinates and attributions returned by Places are transient in memory and are not included in offline downloads. The interactive map supplies Google's own map attribution; fresh Places cards show provider attribution and retrieval time. Review Google's current Maps Platform terms for the deployment and region.

Worldwide place search uses [Photon](https://photon.komoot.io), a geocoder indexing [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) data (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). The picker displays attribution. Only the typed search query is sent to the public service, after a 500 ms debounce; server fetches are cached for a day and successful result responses for an hour. Availability and coverage depend on the public service. Requests time out after four seconds and fall back to bundled cities, with an explicit unavailable notice and a manual map-position path. No paid key or reservation data is sent. Selected names/points are saved as user-selected locations; they are not proof of a provider-verified venue or reservation. Manual coordinates are validated locally on the server, without resolving external links.
